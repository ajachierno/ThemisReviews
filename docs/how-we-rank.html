How we rank smart home gear

Every ranking on ThemisReviews comes from the same process: pull the listings, read them, score them with one formula, and show the formula. Nobody pays to be listed, and no manufacturer sees a page before it goes up.

Where the data comes from

For each page we search Amazon for that device type and protocol, then open every candidate listing and record its title, price, star rating, review count, the "bought in past month" line when Amazon shows one, the seller, and the full feature bullets. Those numbers come straight from the listing on the capture date. We don't edit them, and they drift as Amazon updates prices and reviews.

A listing only makes the page if its own bullets back up the protocol. A plug that says "Matter" in an ad image but not in the listing text doesn't go on the Matter page.

We don't test products by hand

We don't install each device in a lab. Our rankings combine what thousands of buyers report (the rating and how many people left one) with a check of each listing's specs against what matters for that protocol. This page explains exactly what we do instead.

The score

Every product gets a score out of 100:

  • 45% customer rating: the Amazon star rating out of 5.
  • 30% review volume: the number of ratings on a log scale, with full credit at 200 ratings (lower on a few niche pages). Past that, more reviews add nothing, so between well-proven products the rating and features decide. The log scale means going from 10 to 100 reviews counts as much as going from 1,000 to 10,000. Newer listings get credit for having had less time to collect reviews: if a product has been on Amazon for under two years and has at least 30 reviews, its count is scaled up by 24 divided by its age in months (a one-year-old listing's reviews count double), never more than double. Older listings keep their count as is. We use Amazon's “Date First Available” when the listing shows it; most don't, so we estimate the date from the product's Amazon ID, which Amazon assigns in roughly date order (checked against 340 listings with a known date, the estimate is usually within two months).
  • 25% features: a short checklist for that page (for example 800 Series chip, Long Range, and 3-way support on Z-Wave switches). Each item has a weight, and the weights are the same for every product on the page.

Scores only compare products on the same page. An 80 on the Zigbee switch page and an 80 on the Wi-Fi bulb page don't mean the same thing. On the product-type pages, which mix protocols, each product keeps the score it earned on its own protocol page.

Awards come from the scores

Nobody picks the award winners by hand. Each award is a rule, and the product that fits the rule with the highest score wins it:

  • Best Overall is the highest score among products with at least 4.0 stars from at least 30 reviews, so a long feature list can't carry a poorly rated or barely reviewed product to the top award. If nothing on the page clears both, it goes to the highest score.
  • Best Budget is the highest score among the cheapest third of the page, by price per item. Best Premium is the same for the priciest third.
  • Awards for a feature or a system, such as Best Outdoor or Best for Apple Home, go to the highest score among the products with that feature or that work with that system.
  • Most Popular goes to the most Amazon reviews, Best Rated to the highest star rating, and Most Features to the most of the page's scored features.

A product holds one award at most, so the next award goes to the best product that hasn't won one yet. Ties go to the product with more reviews. Awards move on their own when the numbers change, and each award card shows the figures behind it.

Avoid picks

A product lands in "the one to avoid" only for a concrete problem we can point to in the listing, and we quote it. Past examples: a title naming one model while the bullets describe another, an import model that won't work on US power or apps, a reseller charging far more than retail, and a camera that does nothing until you pay for a plan. Being new or having few reviews isn't enough on its own.

Works with

The Works with column shows which smart home systems can control a product, either directly or through a hub the listing says you need. We follow the listing first, then these rules for the protocol:

  • Matter devices work with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, SmartThings, Home Assistant, Hubitat, and Homey, given a Matter controller (and a Thread border router for Matter over Thread).
  • Z-Wave devices work with Home Assistant, SmartThings, Hubitat, and Homey. Alexa, Google, and Apple can't talk to Z-Wave.
  • Zigbee 3.0 devices work with Home Assistant, SmartThings, Hubitat, Homey, and Echo speakers that have a built-in Zigbee hub.
  • Brand-app devices (Wi-Fi and Bluetooth) get the systems their listing names, plus Home Assistant when it has an official integration for that brand.

The "Must work without" filters

Tables and the picker on the home page can hide products that need something you don't have:

  • Neutral wire: only switches, dimmers, outlets, relays, and fan controls whose listing says no neutral is needed. When a listing contradicts itself, we go with the title.
  • Hub: hides anything that needs a separate hub, bridge, gateway, or Thread border router to work. Z-Wave, Zigbee, Thread, and Lutron devices always do. Kits that include their hub count as hub-free, and so do Bluetooth devices you control from your phone.
  • Subscription: hides products whose main job needs a paid plan. For cameras and doorbells that includes saving recordings. Optional extras, like a router's security add-on, don't count.

Gift guides and deal pages

Gift picks come from the same rankings: the best-scoring product in each device type within the price limit, with at least 100 reviews and 4.2 stars. Accessories like replacement sensors and USB sticks are left out.

Updates and corrections

Each page shows when it was last updated and when its Amazon prices were checked. When we refresh a page, a "What changed" note lists products added or dropped, award changes, and corrections. If you spot a mistake, tell us on GitHub and we'll fix it and note the change.

How we make money

Links to Amazon carry our Associates tag. If you buy through one, Amazon pays us a commission at no extra cost to you. That's the only money involved. No brand pays for placement, sends us free products, or reviews a page before it's published.
