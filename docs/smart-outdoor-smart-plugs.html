How do these protocols differ? Show the pros and cons
Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz (most smart devices), 5 GHz (cameras)

Pros

  • No hub; it joins the network you already have
  • Enough bandwidth for live video and audio
  • Widest choice of brands and the lowest entry prices
  • Setup is usually an app and a few taps

Cons

  • Power hungry; battery Wi-Fi sensors need frequent charging
  • Many devices only support 2.4 GHz, which complicates setup on mixed-band routers
  • Dozens of devices can overload consumer routers
  • Many products depend on the maker's cloud and stop working if it shuts down
  • No mesh between devices; range is whatever your router covers

Our take: Right for cameras, doorbells, and anything plugged in. Wrong for battery sensors, and a few dozen devices can overload a cheap router.

Matter Wi-Fi, Thread, or Ethernet

Pros

  • One device works with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, SmartThings, and Home Assistant
  • Multi-admin lets several ecosystems control the same device at once
  • Local control by design, no cloud needed for day-to-day use
  • Setup is a QR code scan in whichever app you prefer

Cons

  • Only device types in the current spec are supported, so advanced features often stay in the maker's app
  • Each platform exposes a different slice of what a device can do
  • Pairing and multi-admin bugs are still common
  • Matter over Thread devices still need a Thread border router

Our take: Buy Matter devices when you can, because they keep your options open. Check that the features you need are in the spec before you buy.
